Creative brief: Canton Fair Sourcing Trip, Tour 1

This document briefs the production crew and the WorldFirst teams on the content to be made during Tour 1 of the China Sourcing Trip (Electronics and Homeware), 12 to 14 October 2026, Guangdong. It defines the five deliverables, the shooting plan for each day, the technical specification, the interview guides, the brand rules and the delivery schedule.

Owner: Steven Chen (Content) · On-site lead: Cathy Gao · Version 1.0 · 4 October 2026 · Shared with internal teams and production partners
01

What this covers

WorldFirst runs two three-day factory tours for its clients around the 140th Canton Fair, together with 1688. Tour 1 covers electronics and homeware. Tour 2 (28 to 30 October) covers fashion and beauty and is briefed separately. This brief covers the content produced on Tour 1 only.

01Project
China Sourcing Trip 2026, Tour 1: Electronics and Homeware. Factory visits in Foshan and Dongguan, a selection centre visit in Shenzhen, and a closing workshop at the Alibaba Center.
02Dates
Day 0 arrival Sunday 11 October. Shooting days Monday 12 to Wednesday 14 October 2026. Confirm the final day-by-day schedule with Doris Yang before departure.
03Group
Up to 35 clients on one 42-seat coach, with WorldFirst staff. Realistic attendance is 10 to 20 clients. Mostly Australia and New Zealand based importers, with clients from Singapore, the UK and Thailand.
04Partners
1688 (Alibaba's domestic B2B wholesale platform) provides the factory network, the selection centre and the workshop venue. 1688 sales and operations staff attend. 1688 marketing participation and co-branding of the content are not yet confirmed.
05Readers of this brief
Production agency (videographer, photographer, editor). WorldFirst content team (Steven Chen, Cathy Gao). ANZ team on the tour (Doris Yang, Joshua Neudecker). Project lead Julia and 1688 liaison Aria for the venue and partner items.
06What is new this year
Last spring's trip produced photography and a few interviews only. This year the whole trip is filmed as one episode of a recurring series, with a named host, a teaser, a B-roll library, short-form cut-downs and staged testimonials on the final day.
02

Objectives and measures

The content has one primary objective and two supporting ones. Every shooting and editing decision should be checked against the primary objective first.

01Primary objective
Generate registrations and interest for the next China Sourcing Trip. Measured by sign-ups and enquiries for the next tour, and by the number of people who ask about WorldFirst because of the episode. The episode is also the recruitment asset sent to prospects for future trips.
02Supporting objective 1
Position WorldFirst as the payments company that takes its clients to the source. No other payments provider runs sourcing trips for its users. Viewers who never join a trip should still come away trusting WorldFirst with payments to China.
03Supporting objective 2
Build the Sourcing Trip as a recurring WorldFirst property tied to the Canton Fair, so that each fair produces a new episode of the same show.
04Campaign context
The wider Canton Fair campaign targets 1,000,000 impressions for 'Pay to China' awareness content across WorldFirst channels. Tour 1 content is one input to that figure.
05Social measure
Organic engagement on the episode and teaser: views, watch time, saves, shares and comments on WorldFirst channels.
06What the content is not
Not a brand montage. Not an event aftermovie. Not a product advertisement. Viewers should learn what sourcing in China looks like on the ground and what it is like to travel with WorldFirst.
03

Audience

The viewer is a cross-border importer or e-commerce seller who has not yet sourced directly from Chinese factories, or has done so only through intermediaries.

PRIMARY

Importers in Australia and New Zealand

  • Owner-operators and small teams selling homeware, appliances and consumer electronics online and through retail.
  • Annual GMV typically US$1 million to US$5 million.
  • Have heard of the Canton Fair and 1688 but have not visited factories themselves.
  • Want lower minimum order quantities, samples, category insight and reliable suppliers.
SECONDARY

Importers in Southeast Asia, the UK and Europe

  • Same profile in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, the UK and Germany.
  • Some are repeat Canton Fair visitors who have never been inside a factory.
  • Interested in OEM and ODM customisation and overseas stock options.
TERTIARY

Existing WorldFirst clients

  • Clients who already pay suppliers through WorldFirst and may join a future tour.
  • Clients who could provide testimonials for lifecycle email and the website.
PEOPLE ON THE TOUR

What the participants told us

  • Mix of first-time China visitors and people who have attended the Canton Fair more than three times.
  • Categories: home appliances, consumer electronics, homeware, with some beauty.
  • Asked for: competitive pricing, OEM and ODM capability, lower minimum order quantities, free samples, category analysis reports.
  • Several need certifications (CE, UKCA, LFGB, CB test reports). Supplier conversations about certification are worth recording.
Participant names, companies and contact details are held by the WorldFirst ANZ team and are not part of this brief. Ask Doris Yang on site before filming any client in close-up, and record a verbal consent on camera.

The show: WorldFirst Sourcing Trip Diaries

The trip is filmed as one episode of a recurring series. A named host carries the viewer through the three days. The crew works with the host, not around the host.

01Series name
WorldFirst Sourcing Trip Diaries. Working episode title: 'Sourcing Trip Diaries: Canton Fair 2026, Electronics and Homeware'. Whether '1688' appears in the title depends on the co-branding decision (see open items).
02Host
Joshua Neudecker, ANZ Marketing Manager, WorldFirst. He is visiting Chinese factories for the first time and knows the WorldFirst product. He is not presented as a sourcing expert or an influencer. He reacts, asks questions and summarises what he learns.
03Second voice
Doris Yang, ANZ Partnerships, coordinates the tour and carries the China know-how. She is not the host and should not be asked to manage the crew. She appears when she explains something to Joshua or to the group.
04Story shape
Day 1: arrival, first factory, first impressions, welcome dinner. Day 2: deeper factory visits, supplier conversations, what buyers are learning. Day 3: the selection centre, the workshop, participants reflecting on what changed for them. Each day ends with a short piece to camera from Joshua.
05Tone
Curious, direct, specific. Real reactions over polished lines. Humour is welcome when it happens. No scripted enthusiasm. Professional but visibly enjoyable, so that viewers want to join the next tour.
06Value to the viewer
Every segment should give the viewer something they can use: how a factory is organised, what buyers ask suppliers, how minimum order quantities are negotiated, how certification is handled, how samples work, how payment to the supplier is made. Entertainment carries the value, it does not replace it.
07Payment moments
Where a client pays or discusses paying a supplier through WorldFirst, record it. If any visited factory already receives payments through WorldFirst, film the buyer and the supplier in the same frame talking about it. Confirm supplier status with Doris Yang before raising it on camera.
08Reference
Travel and workplace documentary formats where a host learns on camera, for example https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zt3F7kRB5ik. The reference is for pace and host behaviour, not for visual style.
09Distribution
The episode and teaser are published on WorldFirst channels (YouTube, LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok). Joshua Neudecker has no personal distribution channel and nothing is planned for his own accounts.

Shooting plan by day

The agenda below is the planning version from the project kick-off and is subject to change. Doris Yang holds the live schedule. The crew is on site every day from the first departure to the end of the evening programme.

Day 0, Sunday 11 October: arrival

  • Optional: short arrival pieces at the hotel. Joshua Neudecker records a 30-second opening piece to camera: who he is, where he is, what he expects.
  • Crew checks: batteries, cards, mics paired and labelled, release forms printed, shot list reviewed with Cathy Gao.

Day 1, Monday 12 October: household homeware

  • Morning: one homeware factory. Afternoon: two homeware factories. Foshan or Dongguan, one to two hours from Guangzhou.
  • Coach: Joshua and Doris talk about what is coming. Doris explains the factory cluster. Mics on both.
  • At each factory: the arrival and greeting, the factory manager's walk-through, the production line, the showroom, and at least one client asking the supplier a real question. Ask the client and supplier to sit or stand together for the camera when a good conversation is happening.
  • Team uniform day: WorldFirst branded clothing is worn on Day 1 at the factories and the welcome dinner. Make the most of branded wide shots on this day.
  • Evening: welcome dinner. Toasts, the group meeting each other, food, any tea ceremony performance. Joshua's end-of-day piece to camera after dinner.

Day 2, Tuesday 13 October: household appliances and consumer electronics

  • Morning: one appliance factory. Afternoon: two consumer electronics factories.
  • Priority: the deeper supplier conversations. Clients now know what to ask. Record negotiations on minimum order quantity, samples, certification, lead times and payment terms. Record how the supplier wants to be paid and whether WorldFirst is mentioned.
  • Record a client checking a payment or a quote on their phone or laptop. If a client pays a supplier through WorldFirst during the tour, film it with the client's permission.
  • Joshua's end-of-day piece to camera. Evening is free time; film the group only if something natural happens.

Day 3, Wednesday 14 October: Shenzhen and the Alibaba Center

  • Morning: 1688 consumer electronics selection centre, Shenzhen. Products, trends, clients browsing and reacting. Joshua compares what he sees with the factories.
  • Afternoon, Alibaba Center workshop. Before the educational content, Doris Yang runs a round where every participant shares an 'aha moment' from the first two days and, for first-time visitors, what China was like. Film the whole round with two cameras and mics on every speaker.
  • Workshop sessions: 1688 introduction, 1688 and WorldFirst product introduction, WorldFirst introduction, dropshipper session, networking. Record for context and B-roll; do not film long stretches of slides.
  • After the workshop: the staged interviews on the dressed set (sections 07 and 08). Clients first, then partners. Allow 10 to 15 minutes per client and 20 to 30 minutes per partner.
  • Optional close: participants joining the Canton Fair party in the evening, if confirmed. A short closing piece from Joshua.
07

Technical specification

One videographer, one photographer, plus a stage set-up on Day 3. The specification exists so that landscape footage can be reframed for portrait without a second camera.

01Video camera
One cinema or mirrorless camera recording 16:9 at 4K (UHD 3840 x 2160) minimum, 25p, 10-bit log where available. Framing leaves headroom and side room so that a 9:16 crop is possible on every piece to camera and interview. No separate portrait camera.
02Audio
At least four wireless lavalier or clip microphones with a multi-channel receiver, so that three- and four-way conversations are recorded on separate channels. Joshua Neudecker and Doris Yang keep a mic on throughout each day. Two spare mics for clients and suppliers. One on-camera shotgun as safety. Record 24-bit 48 kHz.
03Factory conditions
Factories are loud and often lit by fluorescent tubes. Bring an LED panel or two for interviews on the floor, ND filters for exteriors, and use the lavaliers rather than the shotgun for speech.
04Stage set, Day 3
One sit-down testimonial set with a clean backdrop (WorldFirst branded or a neutral dark backdrop with the WorldFirst logo), key and fill lighting, two cameras on the subject (wide and tight). If a two-seat conversation set is also built, three cameras. Joshua interviews off camera; his voice is not used in the cut. Confirm room dimensions and power with the Alibaba Center through Aria before Day 3.
05Photography
One photographer covering every location. Deliver JPEG edited stills plus RAW. Mix of wide establishing shots, detail shots of products and hands, candid portraits of clients in conversation, group photos at each factory and at the dinner.
06Logging
Each card is labelled by day and location. The crew keeps a shot log with timecodes for every supplier conversation, client reflection, payment moment and piece to camera. The log is delivered with the footage.
07Language on set
The crew may not speak English. Cathy Gao directs on site; Doris Yang and Joshua Neudecker flag moments worth capturing. The crew should keep the camera running whenever a client and supplier are in conversation, even without a cue.
08Consent
A short release form is signed by every participant on Day 0 or Day 1. Suppliers are asked for permission at the start of each visit. Any person who declines is not filmed in close-up.
08

Interview guide

Four sets of questions: the host's running talking points, the Day 3 'aha moment' round, the client testimonials and the partner interviews. Cathy Gao carries the list on site and prompts Joshua Neudecker when a question has been missed.

Host talking points (used throughout the tour)

  • What is this factory known for, and who does it already make for?
  • What is the minimum order quantity, and what does it take to lower it?
  • How does sampling work: cost, time, who pays?
  • Which certifications does this product need for Australia, the UK or Europe, and who handles them?
  • How long from order to shipment, and what causes delays?
  • How does this supplier want to be paid, in which currency, and what happens on the buyer's side?
  • What surprised you about this factory compared with buying through a platform or an agent?
  • What would you tell someone at home who is thinking about coming next time?

Day 3 'aha moment' round (led by Doris Yang)

  • One thing from the last two days that changed how you think about sourcing.
  • For first-time visitors: what was China like compared with what you expected?
  • One thing you will do differently when you get home.

Client testimonial questions (stage, 10 to 15 minutes each)

  • Trip first: Who are you and what does your business sell? Why did you join this trip? What was the highlight? What did you learn that you could not have learned from home?
  • Then WorldFirst: How long have you used WorldFirst and what do you use it for? Before WorldFirst, what was difficult about paying suppliers or holding accounts? What changed after? What result has that produced for your business?
  • Close: Would you come on the next trip, and who should come with you?
  • Rule: three or four trip questions before any WorldFirst question. Never ten payment questions in a row.

Partner interview questions (stage, 20 to 30 minutes each)

  • A longer, more formal slot for 1688 and other partners. Doris Yang sends the question list to partners in advance.
  • Draft questions: What does your organisation do for overseas buyers? Why work with WorldFirst on this trip? What do buyers get wrong when they first source from China? What should a buyer do in their first 90 days? What does the WorldFirst and 1688 relationship make possible for a buyer?
  • Final partner questions are co-written by Doris Yang, Steven Chen and Cathy Gao before 10 October.

WorldFirst in the story

WorldFirst is the organiser and the payment layer. It appears where it is true, not as an insert.

01Where WorldFirst appears
The branded coach and uniforms on Day 1. The workshop at the Alibaba Center. Any client paying or discussing payment to a supplier. The testimonials. The host, who works for WorldFirst and says so once at the start.
02What WorldFirst does
Clients hold a WorldFirst World Account, collect from marketplaces, convert currency and pay Chinese suppliers directly, including into the supplier's Alipay or bank account. The approved line is: convert your currency with WorldFirst and pay suppliers directly. Do not imply flows WorldFirst does not support.
031688 co-branding
Tour 1 factories are not necessarily 1688 factories. Whether 1688 appears in the episode title and end card is a decision for Julia and Aria. Until confirmed, film 1688 branding where it exists and keep the edit flexible.
04Call to action
Teaser and episode end with an invitation to register interest in the next China Sourcing Trip. The landing URL will be supplied by the WorldFirst content team before the edit.
05Mentions of competitors
Clients may name other payment providers or platforms. Keep the recording; the edit decides.

Brand and style rules

The edit follows the WorldFirst brand system. Assets are supplied by the WorldFirst content team and used as files, never redrawn.

01Logo
WorldFirst logo supplied as SVG and PNG, positive on light grounds, reverse (white) on dark grounds. Never recoloured, stretched or given effects. The gradient belt in the mark stays as supplied. Minimum 24 px high on screen.
02Colour
WF Red #FF0051 is present in every end card and lower third. Supporting colours: WF Dark Purple #13002D, WF Grey #484F56, WF Black #1F2323, WF Light Grey #ECEDEE, white. No second accent colour.
03Typography
Poppins for all on-screen English text (titles Extrabold 800, lower thirds Medium 500). Alibaba PuHuiTi Bold 700 for any Chinese text. No other fonts.
04Subtitles
English subtitles for all Chinese speech and for any English that is hard to hear in factories. White Poppins Medium with a soft dark band. British English spelling. No em-dashes. Currency written as US$3,000 or RMB 20,000.
05Lower thirds
Name and role for every speaker on first appearance. Clients: first name, company, country. Suppliers: company and city. Partners: name, role, organisation.
06Music
Licensed library music only, cleared for YouTube, Meta and TikTok. Mid-tempo and warm. No trap or hard electronic beds. Music sits under natural sound; factory sound stays audible.
07Photography principles
Real people and genuine interaction, warm natural light, business in action, local vibrancy, moments of connection. No heavy colour grading, no high-contrast looks, no oversaturation.
08Writing
Any on-screen copy follows WorldFirst copy rules: British English, sentence case, no exclamation marks, no words such as seamless, world-class, game-changing or cutting-edge.
