Tour 1 field guide: what we capture and how

Three days, five themes, one episode. This guide says what we are trying to get on camera, where each moment sits in the schedule, what it turns into afterwards, and how to work with the crew without turning the trip into a shoot. The production agency has its own brief; this one is for the three of you.

Owner: Steven Chen · On-site lead: Cathy Gao · Version 1.0 · 4 October 2026 · 12 to 14 October, Guangdong
01

What we are making and why

The trip becomes one episode of a recurring show, WorldFirst Sourcing Trip Diaries. Joshua carries the viewer through it as a first-time visitor who knows the WorldFirst product. The point is that people who watch want to be on the next trip, and people who never come still trust WorldFirst with their payments to China.

01The episode
8 to 12 minutes, landscape. The spine of everything. Three days in order, Joshua's pieces to camera as the joins.
02The teaser
Under two minutes, out between Canton Fair Phase 1 and 2. Best moments only. Ends on an invitation to register for the next trip.
03Cut-downs
8 to 12 portrait reels from single moments: a supplier answering a hard question, a client reacting, a negotiation, Joshua's end-of-day summary. Not time sensitive.
04Testimonials
Day 3 sit-downs on the stage at the Alibaba Center. These become standalone client and partner videos, website quotes and lifecycle email assets.
05B-roll
The crew collects this on its own. You do not need to think about it beyond letting them roam.
06The test
Before asking the crew to roll on anything, ask: would a buyer at home learn something, or want to be here? If neither, let it go.
02

Who does what on the ground

Three jobs, clearly separated, so nobody is doing two things at once.

CATHY

Directs the content

  • Decides what the crew films and when. The crew does not speak English; you are their eyes and ears.
  • Carries the theme list and the prompt bank. Nudges Joshua when a theme has not been covered that day.
  • Picks who sits down for testimonials on Day 3, based on who talks well on Days 1 and 2.
  • Runs the five-minute evening check with the crew: what we got, what we are missing tomorrow.
DORIS

Runs the tour, flags the moments

  • You are the coordinator and the China know-how. You are not the host and you do not manage the crew.
  • Keep a mic on. When you explain something to Joshua or to the group, that is content.
  • Point Cathy at the moments: a client about to negotiate, a supplier about to explain something, a first-timer about to react.
  • Own the Day 3 'aha moment' round and the partner interview slot.
JOSHUA

Hosts

  • First-time visitor, knows WorldFirst, not a sourcing expert, not an influencer. React, ask, summarise.
  • One piece to camera per day, 30 to 60 seconds, after the last stop. Plus a 30-second opener on Day 0.
  • Ask suppliers and clients the questions you actually have. The prompt bank is there for when you run dry.
  • On Day 3, you interview off camera. Your voice is not used in the cut, so keep questions short and let the answer breathe.
THE CREW

Films

  • One videographer at 16:9 4K, one photographer, four or more wireless mics.
  • Standing instruction: when a client and a supplier are talking, keep rolling. They will not wait for a cue.
  • They collect B-roll between stops without you.
  • They build the stage on Day 3. Cathy coordinates the room with Aria and Julia.
03

The five themes

Everything we capture belongs to one of five themes. Each theme has a brand need attached. The brand need is one question or one moment, not a line of questioning. If the moment is not there, do not force it.

011. First time inside
Joshua's (and every first-timer's) reaction to the scale, the speed, the organisation of a Chinese factory. Capture: the walk in, the line, the showroom, the honest reaction. Becomes: the episode spine, cut-downs. Brand need: none. This theme earns attention; it does not sell.
022. Buyer meets maker
A client and a supplier working something out: minimum order quantity, samples, certification, lead time, customisation. Capture: the conversation itself, both faces, the moment the answer lands. Becomes: the value segments of the episode, the strongest cut-downs. Brand need: one question about how the supplier wants to be paid, if the conversation gets there on its own.
033. How the money moves
What happens on the buyer's side when a Chinese supplier wants paying. Capture: a client checking a quote or payment on their phone, a supplier saying which currency and account they want, any client who pays through WorldFirst during the trip, a supplier who already receives WorldFirst payments. Becomes: the one payment segment in the episode, B-roll the pay team has asked for, testimonial material. Brand need: this is the brand need. One clean moment is enough for the whole episode.
044. The room
The group becoming a group: coach conversations, the welcome dinner, toasts, the tea ceremony, yum cha, people swapping cards. Capture: wide and candid, no interviews. Becomes: the teaser, the connective tissue of the episode, the recruitment asset. Brand need: branded uniforms on Day 1, WorldFirst visible in the wide shots.
055. What changed
What participants think on Day 3 that they did not think on Day 0. Capture: the 'aha moment' round, the sit-down testimonials, Joshua's closing piece. Becomes: the ending of the episode, standalone testimonials, website and lifecycle quotes. Brand need: in the testimonials, after the trip questions, how WorldFirst fits into their business.
Rule of thumb for the brand need: one payment question per factory at most, and only when a client and supplier are already talking about terms. Twenty questions in a row interrupts the trip and reads as an advert.

Per slot: what is happening, which themes are live, the must-gets, and Joshua's piece-to-camera prompt. Must-gets are the two or three things the episode cannot do without. Everything else is a bonus. The agenda is the planning version; Doris holds the live schedule.

05

How to be on camera

The best material on a trip like this is never planned. The job is to have the camera on when it happens and to stay out of the way.

  • Let conversations happen. Clients and suppliers talk about what matters to them; that is the content. Nobody should feel interviewed on the factory floor.
  • Ask what you actually want to know. Joshua's real questions are better than our list. The prompt bank in section 06 is for when a conversation stalls or a theme has gone uncovered.
  • One brand question, then stop. If payment comes up naturally, one follow-up. If it does not come up, it waits for the stage on Day 3.
  • Asking a client to be filmed: 'This is a great conversation, do you mind if the camera sits in?' If they hesitate, drop it. Cathy keeps a list of who has said yes.
  • Asking a supplier: Doris or the 1688 host asks at the start of each visit. Film floors and products freely; faces of workers only in passing.
  • Mics stay on. Doris and Joshua wear a mic all day. Forget it is there. Anything unusable is cut.
  • Joshua's register: curious, specific, honest about not knowing. 'I had no idea it worked like this' beats any scripted enthusiasm. Humour when it happens.
  • Pieces to camera are one take. If it is not right, do one more and move on. They are joins, not monologues.
  • Evening check, five minutes: Cathy and the crew go through the day against the five themes. Whatever is missing is tomorrow's first priority.
06

Prompt bank

Grouped by theme. Use one when it fits the moment. Never run through them in order, never more than two at a stop. Cathy carries this list; Joshua does not need to memorise it.

Theme 1, first time inside (for Joshua, to the camera or to Doris)

  • What did you expect this to look like, and what does it actually look like?
  • Doris, why is this product made here and not somewhere else?
  • Who does this factory already make for?

Theme 2, buyer meets maker (for clients to ask suppliers, or Joshua if nobody does)

  • What is the smallest order you would take from a new overseas buyer, and what would make you go lower?
  • How does sampling work: how long, who pays, what if I want changes?
  • Which certifications do you already hold for Australia, the UK or Europe, and who handles new ones?
  • What usually goes wrong between order and shipment?

Theme 3, how the money moves (one per factory, only when terms are already being discussed)

  • How do your overseas buyers usually pay you, and in which currency?
  • To a client: when a supplier asks for payment in yuan, what do you do today?
  • To a supplier already receiving WorldFirst payments: what changed for you when buyers started paying this way?

Theme 4, the room (not questions, moments)

  • Two clients from different countries comparing notes. A supplier and a client exchanging WeChat. The first toast. Someone trying a dish for the first time.

Theme 5, what changed (coach on Day 2, the round and the stage on Day 3)

  • What is one thing from yesterday that changed how you think about sourcing?
  • If you were coming back next year, what would you do differently from day one?
  • What would you tell someone at home who is on the fence about coming?
07

Day 3: the round, the testimonials, the partners

The one part of the trip that is set up rather than caught. A dressed set, two cameras, proper light. This is where the testimonials come from, and they are hard to get any other way.

The 'aha moment' round (Doris)

  • Before the educational content starts. Everyone speaks, one to two minutes each. Doris asks the two questions and keeps it moving.
  • Two cameras, one wide on the room and one on the speaker. A mic reaches every speaker before they start.
  • Doris names each speaker before they talk, so the edit has the name on audio.

Client testimonials (Cathy picks, Joshua asks off camera, 10 to 15 minutes each)

  • Trip first, three or four questions: who are you and what do you sell, why did you come, what was the highlight, what did you learn that you could not have learned from home.
  • Then WorldFirst, three questions, only if they are a client: what do you use WorldFirst for, what was hard before, what changed and what has it meant for the business.
  • Close: would you come again and who should come with you.
  • Joshua stays off camera and quiet after each question. Short questions, long answers. Nod, do not talk over.
  • Three strong testimonials beat eight thin ones. Cathy chooses from who spoke well on Days 1 and 2.

Partner interviews (Doris, 20 to 30 minutes each)

  • A longer, more formal slot. Doris sends partners the questions in advance; the text below can go as is.
  • Partner answers feed the episode, a standalone partner video and the co-marketing material with 1688.
Partner invitation text for Doris to forward
Hello, as part of the China Sourcing Trip on 14 October we are recording short interviews at the Alibaba Center after the workshop, and we would like to include you. It takes 20 to 30 minutes on a simple set with two cameras. The conversation covers five areas, and you are welcome to prepare notes:

1. What your organisation does for overseas buyers.
2. Why you chose to work with WorldFirst on this trip.
3. What buyers most often get wrong when they first source from China.
4. What a buyer should do in their first 90 days of sourcing directly.
5. What the relationship between WorldFirst and 1688 makes possible for a buyer.

The recording will be used in the trip episode on WorldFirst channels and may be cut into a short standalone video. We will share the edit with you before it goes out. Please let us know if you are happy to take part.
